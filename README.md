## The Three Ways 9Proxy Charges You

Everything on the pricing page falls into one of these buckets, and the bucket you pick determines how your bill behaves:

- **IP-based packages** — a fixed number of residential IPs, unlimited bandwidth on each. Good when you keep long sessions open, pull heavy pages, or simply don't know how much data a job will burn.
- **GB-based packages** — you buy traffic, not IPs. Requests rotate through the pool and you spend gigabytes. Good when each request is small but you need fresh IPs constantly.
- **Bundle packages** — IPs plus a GB allowance in one purchase, priced below buying the two separately.

All three run on a balance model. You aren't renewing a subscription each month; you top up when the balance runs low. That single design choice explains most of the pricing quirks below, including why unused IPs don't vanish on you.

## A Worked Comparison: Per-IP or Per-GB?

This is the decision people actually get stuck on, and it comes down to bytes per request rather than to which headline number looks smaller.

Say your job is pulling product pages at scale. A typical page including images and JSON responses might run a few hundred kilobytes. At 250 KB per request, a 200 GB pack covers roughly **800,000 requests** and costs $200. If you instead buy 500 IPs at $72 with unlimited bandwidth, that same 800,000 requests costs you $72 — as long as 500 simultaneous identities are enough for the target site.

Flip the scenario. Your job is checking geo-availability across 90 countries with tiny API calls, maybe 20 KB each. Now a gigabyte stretches to 50,000 checks, the 5 GB starter pack covers 250,000 of them for $15, and there's no reason to pay for permanent IP slots you'll never fill.

The rough rule: heavy pages and long sessions favour IP packages; light requests and constant rotation favour GB packages. If you genuinely need both in the same project, that's exactly what a bundle is for.

Also note that IP packages never expire, while GB traffic mostly does. If you have an unpredictable workload with long gaps between runs, buying IPs and sitting on them costs you nothing.
